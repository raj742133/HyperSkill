# Research: Tide

Status: written after the build, to run the HyperSkill pipeline on a finished example. It says what is and is not known. It does not claim demand it has not seen.

## Problem, user and job
People who work in timed blocks (the Pomodoro habit: 25 minutes of focus, 5 of rest) need a timer that is already open in a browser tab, shows how much time is left at a glance, and tells them clearly when to stop. The job: "keep me in one block of work without checking the clock, and tell me when to rest." Target user: a student or office worker at a laptop or phone. Tide is also a showcase of a well-tested animated interface, which is a stated goal of the owner, not a market claim.

## Alternatives (3, including doing nothing)
| Alternative | What it gives | Where it falls short for this job | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pomofocus, a free browser Pomodoro timer | Customisable focus and break times, task estimates, reports; free tier covers the core, a paid tier adds project tracking and integrations (third-party reviews disagree on the exact split) | More features than the job needs; tasks and reports are extra to learn | https://pomofocus.io/ , https://toolradar.com/tools/pomofocus |
| A kitchen or phone timer | Zero software; it is how the technique began (a tomato-shaped kitchen timer) | No modes, no visual sense of time left, you restart it by hand | https://edshare.gcu.ac.uk/5329/2/images/The_Pomodoro_Technique.pdf , https://officetime.net/blog/tomatoes-and-productivity/ |
| Do nothing (watch the clock, work until tired) | No cost | Blocks blur; breaks get skipped | n/a |

## Evidence of demand
**None collected.** A search for evidence on the 25/5 structure found mostly practitioner guides and personal accounts and no controlled study; one source itself says the right length varies by person. Tide therefore makes no claim that Pomodoro works. Lengths are editable (1 to 120 minutes focus, 1 to 60 for breaks). Real demand would need user interviews or usage numbers, which this environment cannot get (gate waived as "human").

## Risks and cheap tests
| Risk | Cheap test |
|---|---|
| The timer drifts or loses time in a sleeping tab | Deadline timestamps, not a counter; a test jumps the clock five minutes and checks the remainder (done) |
| The animation is too heavy for phones | Measured about 14 frames per second in software-rendered Chromium; a real phone is untested (open) |
| Users cannot see or hear that time is up | The tab title shows the time and then "Time is up" (it stays visible when the page is in the background), a screen-reader announcement, an optional chime started from the click that starts the session. A frozen background tab or a phone in a pocket may delay all three; no notification or worker is used (open, a product choice) |
| The computer's clock is changed during a session | Time left is capped at the session's full length, so a clock set back cannot extend it; a clock set forward ends it early. Wall-clock time is used on purpose so a laptop that sleeps still ends on time (done, with unit tests) |
| Colour and motion are hard to read or cause discomfort | Contrast test per colour world, reduced-motion path with no animation loop, axe in every mode (done) |
| Licence or asset problems | No third-party fonts, images or sounds (the chime is generated by the browser) |

## Recommendation
**Go, as a small free example** (the owner asked for it and confirmed it by asking for it to be tested, merged and deployed). Not a claim of market demand.
